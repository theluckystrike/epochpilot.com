What Is the Cron Expression for Every 10 Minutes?

*/10 * * * *

Expression Breakdown

The cron expression */10 * * * * schedules a job to run every 10 minutes. A standard cron expression consists of five fields representing minute, hour, day of month, month, and day of week. Here is what each field means in this expression:

Minute*/10 — every 10th minute (0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50)
Hour* — every hour
Day of Month* — every day of the month
Month* — every month
Day of Week* — every day of the week

This schedule runs 144 times per day (6 times per hour), which amounts to 1008 times per week and approximately 4320 times per month.

Code Examples

Linux / macOS Crontab

# Open crontab editor
crontab -e

# Add this line to run your script every 10 minutes
*/10 * * * * /usr/local/bin/my-task.sh

# With logging
*/10 * * * * /usr/local/bin/my-task.sh >> /var/log/my-task.log 2>&1

Python (schedule library alternative)

# Using APScheduler with cron trigger
from apscheduler.schedulers.blocking import BlockingScheduler
from apscheduler.triggers.cron import CronTrigger

scheduler = BlockingScheduler()
trigger = CronTrigger.from_crontab("*/10 * * * *")
scheduler.add_job(my_function, trigger)
scheduler.start()

JavaScript (Node.js with node-cron)

const cron = require('node-cron');

// Run task every 10 minutes
cron.schedule('*/10 * * * *', () => {
  console.log('Task running at', new Date().toISOString());
  // Your task logic here
});

Frequently Asked Questions

What does each field in */10 * * * * mean?

A cron expression has five fields separated by spaces: minute (0-59), hour (0-23), day of month (1-31), month (1-12), and day of week (0-7, where 0 and 7 are Sunday). The */n syntax means "every nth interval." In */10 * * * *, the fields are: */10 — every 10th minute (0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50); * — every hour; * — every day of the month.

How do I verify that */10 * * * * is correct?

Use the EpochPilot Cron Expression Builder to paste your expression and see the next run times visually. You can also use the crontab -l command to list your current crontab entries, or test with a simple echo command: */10 * * * * echo "test" >> /tmp/cron-test.log.

What timezone does cron use?

By default, cron uses the system timezone configured on your server. On most cloud servers this is UTC. You can check your system timezone with timedatectl on Linux or date +%Z. Some cron implementations (like Kubernetes CronJobs) let you specify a timezone explicitly. Always verify your server's timezone to ensure jobs run at the expected times.
